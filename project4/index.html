02 / Recover homographies

One plane, two perspectives

A homography maps a point in one view to its corresponding point in another. The 3 × 3 matrix has eight free parameters after fixing its last entry to 1. At least four point pairs in a non-degenerate configuration are needed; additional pairs form an overdetermined system.

[u, v, w]ᵀ = H [x, y, 1]ᵀ x′ = u / w y′ = v / w

For each correspondence, computeH adds two equations to A h = b and solves with numpy.linalg.lstsq . This minimizes the algebraic residual across the input point pairs.

[x, y, 1, 0, 0, 0, −xx′, −yx′] h = x′ [0, 0, 0, x, y, 1, −xy′, −yy′] h = y′

Inverse warping

The warp first transforms the image corners to determine the output bounds. It then visits every destination pixel, adds the canvas origin offset, and applies H⁻¹ to find its source coordinate. Sampling the source this way avoids the holes that forward pixel mapping can leave behind.

Correspondences and recovered matrices

Matching colors and numbers show corresponding locations in the two images. Each matrix maps the left image into the right image’s coordinates at the 1200-pixel working scale, with h₃₃ = 1.

03 / Warp the images

Rectifying two planar subjects

Both implementations use inverse warping. Nearest-neighbor rounds to the closest source pixel, requiring one sample per destination pixel. Bilinear interpolation computes a weighted average of four neighboring samples. Nearest-neighbor has less sampling work and can show jagged edges or pixel blocks; bilinear requires more arithmetic but produces smoother results, sometimes softening fine detail.

Framed portrait

Here, it is difficult to tell a difference between nearest neighbor and bilinear interpolation. I think this is due to the extreme angle of the portrait, which results in a blurry image either way. There is a lack of detail in the face and background so results are similar.

Emergency-procedures sign

Here, nearest neighbor creates sudden and weird-looking transitions between letters and the background. This can be noticed especially near the bottom right of the image. Bilinear interpolation makes the text much clearer by making the transitions along the letter edges smoother. Here, there is a clear benefit to using bilinear interpolation over nearest neighbor.

04 / Blend images into a mosaic

A larger field of view

I use corresponding points in the two photographs to estimate a homography that aligns the left image with the right image. I then inverse-warp the left image using bilinear interpolation, using the right image as the reference. Both images are placed on a shared canvas large enough to contain their combined views.

To join them smoothly, I use feather blending: pixels near the center of each source image receive more weight than those near its edges. In overlapping regions, I combine the aligned colors using a weighted average. Otherwise, I just take the pixel from the image that covers that location. In this way, I have a smooth transition between the two images.
